Thresholds worth knowing 2026 · what changed is marked

  • Child Tax Credit: $2,200 per child (up to $1,700 refundable)Raised
  • Annual gift exclusion: $19,000 per recipient, per giver
  • Student loan interest: deduct up to $2,500 (phases out $85,000–$100,000 single/HoH · $175,000–$205,000 joint)
  • CTC phase-out: starts at $400,000 joint · $200,000 other (credit falls $50 per $1,000 of MAGI above)
  • Dependent care FSA: $7,500 household · $3,750 MFS (up from $5,000/$2,500 — only if your employer's plan adopts it)Raised
  • Quarterly estimates: generally required if you'll owe $1,000+ after withholding and refundable credits
  • 1099-NEC threshold: $2,000+ (the $600 rule is gone)Changed
  • Business mileage: 72.5¢ Jan–Jun, 76¢ Jul–Dec (changed mid-year)Changed
  • Self-employment tax: 15.3% on 92.35% of net profit (owed once net earnings reach $400)
  • HSA catch-up, 55+: +$1,000 on top of the contribution limits above (self-only or family, either way)

Three five-minute gap checks

Reference only — this page reports the difference and stops there
  1. Find your gap. Circle the bracket your taxable income lands in and write down the dollars left before the next line.
  2. Room remaining. Add up what you've put into your 401(k), IRA, and HSA so far this year. Write each next to its limit above. The difference is room. 401(k) room closes December 31; IRA and HSA room for 2026 stays open until April 15, 2027.
  3. Phase-out check. Circle every range on this page your income lands inside — Roth, Traditional deduction, student loan interest. Inside a range means the rule is partially yours; that's worth knowing before year-end.
