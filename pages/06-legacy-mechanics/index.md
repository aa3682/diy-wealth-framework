# Legacy Mechanics

If you die without a plan, the state has one for you—and it is slow, public, and expensive[span_28](start_span)[span_28](end_span). Basic estate planning ensures your wealth transfers exactly how you intend[span_29](start_span)[span_29](end_span).

## 1. Beneficiary Designations (The Override)
Accounts with named beneficiaries bypass probate entirely[span_30](start_span)[span_30](end_span). This is the fastest way to transfer wealth[span_31](start_span)[span_31](end_span).
* **Action:** Log into every bank, brokerage, and 401(k) account today[span_32](start_span)[span_32](end_span). Ensure you have named Primary and Contingent beneficiaries[span_33](start_span)[span_33](end_span).
* **POD/TOD:** Add a Payable-On-Death (POD) or Transfer-On-Death (TOD) designation to your checking, savings, and taxable brokerage accounts[span_34](start_span)[span_34](end_span).
* *Warning:* Beneficiary designations trump whatever is written in your will[span_35](start_span)[span_35](end_span). Keep them updated after major life events (marriage, divorce, children)[span_36](start_span)[span_36](end_span).

## 2. The Core Documents
Every adult needs these three documents, regardless of net worth[span_37](start_span)[span_37](end_span):
* **Last Will and Testament:** Dictates the distribution of assets not covered by TOD/POD, and crucially, names guardians for minor children[span_38](start_span)[span_38](end_span).
* **Durable Financial Power of Attorney:** Appoints someone to handle your finances, pay bills, and manage assets if you are incapacitated but still alive[span_39](start_span)[span_39](end_span).
* **Advance Healthcare Directive:** Names a proxy to make medical decisions on your behalf and outlines your wishes for end-of-life care[span_40](start_span)[span_40](end_span).

## 3. The Trust Threshold (Will vs. Trust)
A Last Will and Testament guarantees your estate will go through probate court. Probate is public record, costs time, and incurs legal fees. A Revocable Living Trust holds your assets directly, allowing you to bypass probate entirely.
* **When to Upgrade:** You cross the threshold from needing a simple Will to needing a Revocable Living Trust if:
  1. Your net worth is substantial (generally $1M+ outside of retirement accounts).
  2. You own real estate in more than one state (which would trigger multiple probates).
  3. You want to control *how* and *when* beneficiaries receive money (e.g., trickling out funds to children at ages 25, 30, and 35 rather than handing them a lump sum at 18).
