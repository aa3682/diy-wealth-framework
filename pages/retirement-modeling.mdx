# Retirement Modeling & Glide Paths

A glide path dictates how a portfolio's asset allocation shifts from aggressive accumulation to conservative decumulation over an investor's timeline. This framework provides the mechanical targets for managing capacity for risk.

<Callout type="warning">
  **Sequence of Returns Risk (SORR):** The most vulnerable window for a retirement portfolio is the five years immediately preceding and following retirement. Experiencing a severe market drawdown during this decumulation phase mathematically damages the portfolio's survival probability far more than a similar drawdown during early accumulation.
</Callout>

## Baseline Equity Glide Paths

These structural targets map the optimal ratio of equities (growth) to fixed income (preservation) based on age and distance from target retirement.

| Age Band | Equity | Fixed Income | Primary Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **20s to 30s** | 100% | 0% | Maximum compound growth; volatility is an asset. |
| **30s to 40s** | 90% | 10% | Continued aggressive accumulation with baseline ballast. |
| **40s to 50s** | 80% | 20% | Transitioning to wealth preservation; introducing sequence risk mitigation. |
| **50s to 60s** | 70% | 30% | De-risking against market shocks prior to the withdrawal phase. |
| **Retirement** | 60% | 40% | Safe withdrawal rate optimization; balancing inflation protection with capital preservation. |

## The Mechanics of Safe Withdrawal Rates (SWR)

Determining sustainable cash flow requires modeling historical market cycles against modern life expectancies.

### The 4% Rule (The Baseline)
Based on the Trinity Study, a 60/40 portfolio has historically survived a 30-year retirement period when withdrawing 4% of the initial portfolio balance in year one, adjusted annually for inflation. 

### Dynamic Withdrawal Adjustments
A static withdrawal rule is inefficient for self-directed investors. Implementing dynamic guardrails prevents premature portfolio depletion:
1. **Ceiling Rule:** If the portfolio outpaces expected returns by a set margin (e.g., +20% above the initial balance), increase the withdrawal rate by 10% to prevent under-spending.
2. **Floor Rule:** If the portfolio drops below a set margin (e.g., -20% below the initial balance), decrease the withdrawal rate by 10% to defend the principal during market contractions.
3. **Cash Buffer Rule:** Maintain 12 to 24 months of living expenses in cash equivalents (T-Bills, Money Market Funds) to draw from during severe equity drawdowns, avoiding the forced sale of depressed assets.
