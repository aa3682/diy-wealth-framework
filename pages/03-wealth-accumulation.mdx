# Wealth Accumulation

Investing is not about picking the right stock; it is about capturing the broad growth of human productivity over decades. 

## The Hidden Cost of Fees
The financial services industry is built on extracting a percentage of your assets under management (AUM). While a 1% annual fee sounds small, it does not just take 1% of your returns—it permanently removes that capital from your compounding engine. 

Over a 30-year horizon, a 1% fee will systematically confiscate roughly 25% of your terminal wealth. Run your own numbers below:

## Core Portfolio Construction
To avoid fee drag, a DIY investor should focus on low-cost, highly liquid index funds with Expense Ratios under 0.10%. 
* **Equities:** Utilize broad-market ETFs for your core equity exposure. Funds tracking the S&P 500 (like SPY) or the Nasdaq-100 (like QQQ) provide instant diversification across major U.S. sectors and technology leaders.
* **Fixed Income:** Depending on your age and risk tolerance, blend in bond ETFs to smooth out volatility.

## Satellite Positions and Digital Assets
Once your core is established, limit speculative or sector-specific bets to 1-5% of your total portfolio. 
* **Digital Asset Exposure:** If you are incorporating cryptocurrency, use regulated, liquid vehicles rather than self-custody spot holdings if you prefer structural simplicity. Carve out a maximum of 5% from the equity side of your core model. Allocation to spot Bitcoin ETFs (IBIT) or Ethereum ETFs (ETHA) allows you to capture network upside within traditional brokerage accounts.

## The Automation Rule
Do not attempt to time the market. Set your brokerage to auto-invest a fixed dollar amount into your chosen ETFs on the 1st and 15th of every month, regardless of market news.
