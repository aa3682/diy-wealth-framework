# Retirement Runway

Retirement is a mathematical equation, not an age[span_19](start_span)[span_19](end_span). You achieve Financial Independence (FI) when the passive income from your portfolio exceeds your living expenses[span_20](start_span)[span_20](end_span).

## The 25x Rule (The Target)
To determine your FI number, calculate your projected annual expenses in retirement and multiply by 25[span_21](start_span)[span_21](end_span).
* *Example:* If you need $80,000 a year to live comfortably, your FI target is $2,000,000[span_22](start_span)[span_22](end_span).

## The 4% Rule (The Execution)
The 25x rule is the inverse of the 4% Safe Withdrawal Rate[span_23](start_span)[span_23](end_span). Historical modeling suggests that if you withdraw 4% of your portfolio's value in year one of retirement, and adjust that withdrawal amount for inflation each subsequent year, you have a highly statistically significant chance of not running out of money over a 30-year period[span_24](start_span)[span_24](end_span).

## Sequence of Return Risk (The Bond Tent)
The biggest threat to the 4% Rule is experiencing a severe market crash in the first few years of your retirement. Withdrawing living expenses while your portfolio is down forces you to sell assets at a heavy loss, permanently crippling your compounding engine.
* **The Solution:** Build a "Bond Tent." In the 3 to 5 years leading up to retirement, accumulate 2 to 3 years' worth of living expenses in cash or short-term bond ETFs. 
* **The Execution:** If the stock market crashes in year one of your retirement, you stop selling equities entirely. Instead, you draw your living expenses from the Bond Tent, giving your equity portfolio time to recover before you resume standard withdrawals.

## Compounding Mechanics
Time in the market is the heaviest lever you can pull[span_25](start_span)[span_25](end_span).
* A $10,000 investment growing at an annualized 7% doubles roughly every 10 years[span_26](start_span)[span_26](end_span).
* Delaying your investment journey by 10 years cuts your terminal wealth in half, requiring you to save twice as much later in life just to catch up[span_27](start_span)[span_27](end_span).
