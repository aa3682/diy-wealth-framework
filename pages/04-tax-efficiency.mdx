# Tax Efficiency

It is not what you make; it is what you keep. The IRS provides legal frameworks to shelter your capital. Use them in the correct sequence.

## The Account Sequencing Protocol

<Callout type="warning" emoji="⚠️">
  **Execution Note:** Always maximize your employer 401(k) match (Step 1) before routing any capital to a taxable brokerage. It is mathematically impossible to beat a guaranteed 100% return on your money.
</Callout>

Fund your accounts in this specific order to maximize tax advantages:
1. **The Employer Match:** Contribute to your 401(k) or 403(b) exactly up to the company match. This is an immediate 100% return on your money.
2. **Health Savings Account (HSA):** If you are on a High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP), max out your HSA. It is triple-tax-advantaged (tax-deductible contributions, tax-free growth, tax-free withdrawals for medical). 
3. **Roth IRA:** Max out your Roth IRA. You pay taxes on the seed, but the harvest is permanently tax-free.
4. **Max the 401(k):** Go back to your employer plan and fill it to the IRS maximum to reduce your current year taxable income.
5. **Taxable Brokerage:** Once all tax-advantaged space is filled, route excess cash to a standard brokerage account.

## High-Income Strategies

### The Backdoor Roth IRA
If your Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) exceeds the IRS limits to contribute directly to a Roth IRA, you must use the "Backdoor" method. You make a non-deductible contribution to a Traditional IRA, leave it in cash, and immediately convert it to a Roth IRA.

However, if you have *any* existing pre-tax money in a Traditional, SEP, or SIMPLE IRA, the IRS applies the **Pro-Rata Rule**. You cannot choose to only convert the new after-tax money; the IRS forces you to convert a proportional mix, triggering an unexpected tax bill.

### The HSA Receipt-Tracking Strategy
Turn your HSA into a shadow retirement account by leveraging the IRS loophole that places no time limit on medical reimbursements.
* **The Execution:** Pay for all out-of-pocket medical expenses (co-pays, prescriptions, dental) using your standard checking account, leaving the HSA funds completely untouched to compound over decades in the market.
* **The Archive:** Scan and save every medical receipt in a dedicated cloud folder (e.g., Google Drive or a secure Notion database).
* **The Harvest:** In 20 or 30 years, when you need cash for retirement living expenses, you can submit decades' worth of accumulated receipts to your HSA. You will receive a massive, lump-sum reimbursement completely tax-free, while having allowed the underlying capital to grow uninterrupted for the duration of your career.

## Asset Location
Hold tax-inefficient assets (like bonds or high-dividend REITs) inside your IRA or 401(k). Hold tax-efficient assets (like broad market ETFs such as SPY or QQQ) in your taxable brokerage.
