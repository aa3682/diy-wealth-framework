# Wealth Accumulation

Investing is not about picking the right stock; it is about capturing the broad growth of human productivity over decades[span_2](start_span)[span_2](end_span).

## Core Portfolio Construction
A DIY investor should focus on low-cost, highly liquid index funds[span_3](start_span)[span_3](end_span).
* **Equities:** Utilize broad-market ETFs for your core equity exposure[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span). Funds tracking the S&P 500 (like SPY) or the Nasdaq-100 (like QQQ) provide instant diversification across major U.S. sectors and technology leaders[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span).
* **Fixed Income:** Depending on your age and risk tolerance, blend in bond ETFs to smooth out volatility[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span).

## Satellite Positions and Digital Assets
Once your core is established, limit speculative or sector-specific bets to 1-5% of your total portfolio[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span).
* **Digital Asset Exposure:** If you are incorporating cryptocurrency, use regulated, liquid vehicles rather than self-custody spot holdings if you prefer structural simplicity[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span). Allocation to spot Bitcoin ETFs (IBIT) or Ethereum ETFs (ETHA) allows you to capture network upside within traditional brokerage accounts[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span).

## The Automation Rule
Do not attempt to time the market[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span). Set your brokerage to auto-invest a fixed dollar amount into your chosen ETFs on the 1st and 15th of every month, regardless of market news[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span).
